## About the Founder

### From programmer to warehouse floor to AI advocate

My name is **Chawin Intawong**. I am not an AI professor. I am someone who spent 28 years building and running distribution centers across Thailand — and learned AI because the problems on the floor demanded it.

I started as a programmer in 1994, writing code for finance and securities systems. Then I spent nearly three decades in logistics operations: Nestlé, DHL Exel, Samsung, Minor Food Group, Linfox, and Siam Makro (CP Group). I managed budgets of 280 million baht. I built greenfield warehouses from scratch. I relocated 22,000-square-meter facilities with zero operational disruption.

I watched talented warehouse operators struggle with new WMS screens. I saw experienced managers resist SAP eWM because no one taught them why it mattered. The technology was never the problem. The gap was **trust and translation**.

So I started learning AI. Not in a lab. On the job.

At Pattna Intercool, I built a stock-taking app. I linked WMS to SAP. I created an AI procurement system. I built a maintenance app, a business hub, and custom reporting tools. Recently, I completed a **Generative AI Mastermind** to deepen my understanding.

Now I am building the platform I wish existed when I started.

## The Journey

### 1994 — Programmer
Started as a programmer at CL Sahaviriya Finance and Siam City Syndicate Finance. Learned that code is only useful if it solves a real problem.

### 1998 — IT Project Manager
Led WMS implementations for Johnson & Johnson across Thailand, Malaysia, and Taiwan. First exposure to warehouse software at scale.

### 2001 — Operations Manager, DHL Exel
Managed warehouse and transport for Nestlé, Siam Makro, Minor Group, and ALCAN. Delivered SAP L.E.S. for Nestlé's 40,000 sqm DC. Secured a 240 million baht contract renewal.

### 2007 — Senior Manager, CEVA & Toll
Led Samsung Thailand DC relocation (22,000 sqm, Laem Chabang) with zero disruption. Built Western Digital's Finished Goods Hub and Huawei's Telecommunication Distribution Hub.

### 2012 — Site Manager, Linfox / Minor Food Group
Full P&L ownership of a multi-temperature DC (-25°C to ambient) and transport for 1,200+ QSR stores across 6 brands. Annual logistics budget: 280 million baht. Re-engineered the entire supply chain with SAP eWM and SAP MRP.

### 2014 — Senior Manager, Siam Makro (CP Group)
Directed Thailand's most advanced automated cold-chain DC: an ASRS frozen warehouse with 25,000 pallet positions plus an 18,000 sqm fresh food cross-dock — one of Asia's most complex distribution center builds. Later led dry/non-food DC operations across 3 facilities with full 3PL governance and multi-million baht P&L ownership.

### 2025 — Senior Consultant, Pattna Intercool
Warehouse standardisation, SAP eWM process optimisation, and KPI framework design for commercial freezer manufacturing. Built 7 internal apps including AI procurement, maintenance tracking, and a business hub.

### 2026 — Founder, Pathwise AI
Turning decades of operational experience into a free AI literacy platform for every Thai, every age.

## Why I Built This

At Siam Makro, I watched a 50-year-old warehouse supervisor stare at a new SAP screen for twenty minutes, too proud to ask for help. He had 30 years of operational wisdom. The system treated him like a beginner.

At Minor Food Group, I saw a talented operations manager resist a new WMS because the training was in English, full of jargon, and assumed he already understood "cloud architecture." He didn't. Neither did I, at first.

In Khon Kaen, I helped a friend build a distribution center from 1,200 sqm to 10,000 sqm. He had never run a warehouse before. We learned together. Today that center generates nearly 2 million baht in monthly profit. But the first six months were terrifying — not because the work was hard, but because no one had translated the knowledge into language he trusted.

**That is the gap Pathwise AI fills.**

Not between Thailand and Silicon Valley. Between **what AI can do** and **what Thai people believe they can do**.

This platform is for:
- The parent who wants to guide their child safely through ChatGPT
- The SME owner who thinks AI is only for big companies
- The warehouse supervisor who has seen three "digital transformation" projects fail
- The 60-year-old who thinks technology passed them by
- The 20-year-old who wants a career in AI but has no roadmap

**Free. In Thai. For every age.**

Because digital literacy is not a luxury. It is infrastructure.

## My AI Learning Journey

I did not study AI at university. I studied Statistics and Computer Science at Kasetsart University, then spent decades in warehouses.

My AI journey started with a simple question: *"Can AI help me order freezer parts more efficiently?"*

It turned into an AI procurement system.

Then I asked: *"Can AI help my team find answers faster?"*

It turned into a Business Hub with an AI assistant.

Recently, I completed a **Generative AI Mastermind** to deepen my technical understanding. I am still learning. I will always be learning.

If I can learn AI at 50+ — between running operations, managing budgets, and building apps — so can you. The difference is not talent. The difference is having a path that respects your time, your language, and your experience.
